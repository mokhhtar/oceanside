The Water Hardness Scale — And What It Does to Your Hair

Water hardness is measured in milligrams per litre of calcium carbonate (mg/L = ppm). The higher the number, the more minerals are present — and the more aggressively they coat your hair with every wash.

Classification Range (ppm) Hair & Scalp Impact Action Required
Soft 0 – 60 ppm Minimal. Hair lathers freely and retains moisture. Color treatments last as expected. Standard daily shampoo. Optional shower filter for trace chlorine.
Moderately Hard 61 – 120 ppm Gradual mineral buildup. Mild dullness, frizz, and slightly faster color fade. Chelating shampoo once per week. Shower filter recommended.
Hard 121 – 180 ppm Active mineral coating. Dry, brittle strands, scalp buildup, noticeable color fade (30–40%). Chelating shampoo 2–3× per week. Shower filter strongly recommended.
Very Hard 181+ ppm Severe damage: chronic dryness, breakage, scalp calcification, rapid color oxidation. Chelating shampoo every wash. Shower filter is essential. Monthly scalp detox.

Our database is compiled from USGS water-quality surveys, EPA drinking water reports, and municipal utility disclosures. Values represent typical hardness for a city's main water source and may vary slightly by neighbourhood or season. For the most precise reading, you can request your annual Consumer Confidence Report (CCR) directly from your utility company.
When a ZIP code isn't in our local database, the tool uses the free OpenStreetMap geocoding API to find your latitude and longitude, then shows data from the nearest city we do have on record. This is a reliable approximation, especially for suburban areas that share a regional water system with a tracked city. A yellow banner will appear to let you know when this fallback is being used.
Chelating shampoos contain agents like EDTA, citric acid, or phytic acid that bind to positively-charged mineral ions (calcium, magnesium, iron) and lift them off the hair shaft. Regular shampoos use surfactants designed for oil and organic residue — they cannot break the ionic bonds minerals form with keratin proteins in hair. A chelating shampoo is the only over-the-counter product that can truly clear mineral buildup.
Yes — a good shower filter (KDF-55, Vitamin C, or carbon-based) reduces the mineral and chlorine content of your water before it ever contacts your hair. This means less buildup accumulates with each wash, making your chelating shampoo more effective and extending the results. For Very Hard water areas (180+ ppm), a shower filter is the single highest-impact preventive step you can take.
Most chelating shampoos are safe for color-treated hair when used at the recommended frequency (1–3 times per week). Hard water minerals actually accelerate color fade by disrupting the cuticle, so removing buildup helps preserve your color. Follow any chelating wash with a color-safe conditioner to replenish moisture. Options like Malibu C Hard Water Shampoo are specifically formulated to be color-safe.
